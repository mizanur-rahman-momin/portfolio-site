The best prospects are often already talking in public.

Instead of searching for people to message, read the places where your buyers
already spend time. When you reply with something useful, the conversation
starts on its own.

## Why search feels easy but works slowly

Search gives you a list of names. A list is not a conversation.

You still have to find a reason to reach out. Most messages fail because there
is no reason beyond "I saw your profile".

Communities solve that. They show you what people are working on and what they
are stuck on.

## Where to look

You do not need many places. A few good ones are enough.

- Groups where your buyers ask questions.
- Comment sections under posts from people in your market.
- Posts where someone describes a problem you can help with.
- Events and live sessions where people introduce themselves.

Pick two or three. Read them for a week before you post anything.

## How to take part

The goal is to be useful, not to sell.

1. Read more than you post.
2. Answer a question with a real answer.
3. Add an example from your own work.
4. Ask a short follow-up question.
5. Only mention what you do when it is relevant.

<Callout type="note" title="One rule that keeps you honest">
If your comment would still be useful with your product link removed, you are
doing it right.
</Callout>

## From comment to conversation

A useful comment often gets a reply. That reply is your opening.

Keep the next message short and specific. Reference what they said. Ask one
clear question. Do not pitch in the first message.

## Build a small habit

You do not need an hour a day. Fifteen minutes is enough.

- Ten minutes reading and replying.
- Five minutes noting who is worth a direct message.
- One or two real conversations a week.

Over a month, that becomes a steady flow of warm contacts.

## What to avoid

- Copying the same comment everywhere.
- Pitching in the first reply.
- Arguing in public.
- Adding people to a list and messaging them with a template.

These get short-term attention and long-term silence.
