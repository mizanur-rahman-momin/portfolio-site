A won deal is not the end of the process. It is the template.

Most teams treat a win as a result. The better move is to treat it as a
pattern. Write down what happened, then turn it into steps you can repeat.

## Why wins get forgotten

When a deal closes, everyone moves on. The details stay in someone's memory.

A month later, nobody can say exactly why it worked. So the next deal starts
from zero again.

The fix is simple and a little boring: write it down while it is fresh.

## Look back at the deal

Ask four questions.

1. Where did this person first show up?
2. What problem did they describe?
3. What message got a reply?
4. What made them decide?

The answers are the raw material for your system.

## Turn answers into steps

A system is just a sequence you can repeat.

- **Source.** Where did the contact come from?
- **Qualify.** What made them a good fit?
- **Reach out.** What message worked?
- **Follow up.** How many times, and how?
- **Close.** What removed the last doubt?

Write one line for each. That is your process.

<Callout type="note" title="Keep it short">
If the process needs a long document, it will not be used. One page is enough.
</Callout>

## Remove the manual work

Once the steps are clear, look for what repeats.

- Copying names into a spreadsheet.
- Writing the same first line.
- Remembering to follow up.
- Updating a status by hand.

These are good candidates for automation. A tool like n8n can move data, send
reminders and keep a list tidy. You keep the judgement.

## Test it on the next deal

Run the system on the next few deals. Change what does not work.

- If replies are low, the message needs work.
- If people drop off, the follow-up is too slow.
- If the list is messy, the research step is weak.

Each deal makes the system better.

## A simple rule

If you do something twice the same way, write it down. If you do it three
times, automate it.

That habit turns one good deal into a steady process.
