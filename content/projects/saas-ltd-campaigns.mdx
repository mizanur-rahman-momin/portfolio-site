## Overview

I work with SaaS founders to introduce their products to a lifetime-deal
community. The community is made up of people who like to try new tools early
and give honest feedback.

The main format is a giveaway campaign: I present the product, the community
asks questions, and winners receive accounts or codes from the founder.

## Problem

A new SaaS product often has a good team and a useful tool but no audience.
Paid ads are expensive and hard to measure. The founder needs early users and
real feedback, not just traffic.

On the other side, the community wants deals they can trust and products that
are worth their time.

## Context

- The community is focused on lifetime deals, reviews and giveaways.
- Members expect a clear description of the product and its real use cases.
- Founders need to see interest and feedback, not only signups.

## Goals

1. Introduce a product honestly, with its real benefits and limits.
2. Start a conversation between the founder and the community.
3. Run the campaign in a way that can be repeated for other products.

## How a campaign runs

1. Prepare a product description and the main benefits.
2. Show use cases so members can see where the product fits.
3. Post the campaign and invite questions.
4. The founder answers questions and replies to feedback.
5. Winners are selected and the founder provides accounts or codes.

Each step has a template, so a new campaign does not start from scratch.

## Challenges

- **Trust.** A giveaway only works if the community believes the product is
  real and the rules are fair.
- **Clear rules.** Winners, prizes and timing have to be agreed before the
  campaign starts.
- **After the campaign.** Interest fades quickly. The founder needs a plan for
  the people who showed up.

## Lessons learned

- An honest product description gets better questions and better members.
- A repeatable format saves time and keeps quality consistent.
- The founder's own answers do more for trust than any promotion copy.

LinkedIn, community management, email.
